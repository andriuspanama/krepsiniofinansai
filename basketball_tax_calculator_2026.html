All figures are 2026 estimates for orientation, not tax advice — always verify with a local advisor. Exchange rates: 53.6 TRY/€, 3.95 NIS/€, 117.35 RSD/€ (mid-July 2026). Employee social security is deducted from gross to reach net; employer social security is added on top of gross to reach total club cost. France social security uses a capped PASS/8×PASS model; the AT/MP (workplace accident) component is a rough placeholder since it genuinely varies by club. Spain SS is capped at €61,214.40/yr (2026) plus a small uncapped solidarity surcharge above that. Serbia's mandatory annual surtax is now included directly in the tax figure, modeled with the under-40 exemption. Israel National Insurance uses the 2025 ceiling and threshold (2026 values not confirmed). Lithuanian athlete tax bands follow the VMI 2026 table; Spanish foral (Basque) figures follow the 2026 scale with no athlete-specific reduction unless you enter one.

  1. 32
    Update, 21 Oct 2026Italy — impatriate eligibility flagged. Italian commentary says that since 2024 athletes no longer have a sports-specific route and must meet the general “high qualification” test (a degree or certified higher qualification) plus 3 years of prior non-residence. The Impatriati option is kept but labelled “only if eligible” and the note says to use the local option unless an adviser confirms.
  2. 31
    Update, 21 Oct 2026France — impatriate regime corrected. Three independent sources describe a 30% flat exemption (or a contract-fixed bonus up to a 50% cap) for up to 8 years, with no 2024 reform to 50%/5 years. The option now uses 30% of net pay exempt. Also fixed the French taxable base: it previously deducted a flat 23.5% of gross for social contributions, far above the real deductible share at high salaries; it now uses the actual deductible employee contributions.
  3. 30
    Update, 21 Oct 2026Tax base now net of employee social contributions (Spain, Germany, Italy, Greece, Turkey, France). Several countries applied the income-tax scale to raw gross although employee social contributions are deductible, which overstated tax (and gross) by roughly 2–7% of pay at mid-range salaries. Germany now uses taxable income (work-expense allowance and deductible pension/health/care contributions) with the correct solidarity phase-in; Spain deducts social security and the €2,000 expense allowance and credits the €5,550 personal minimum; Italy and Greece deduct employee contributions; Turkey deducts SGK above the withholding threshold. Serbia, Lithuania and Israel were checked and left unchanged. The Turkey overlap zone moved accordingly.
  4. 29
    Update, 21 Oct 2026Serbia — simplified to one option. The “newly-settled foreign player (Art.15v)” option was removed: the relief needs an indefinite-term employment contract and a job requiring special vocational education, which sports contracts (fixed-term, up to 5 years) probably do not meet, and none of the sources mention athletes. Residents and foreigners are taxed the same on an employment contract. Athletes engaged under a non-employment sports contract are taxed differently (20% withheld on gross minus 50% standardised expenses, about 10% effective, plus contributions); this is noted but not modelled.
  5. 28
    Update, 20 Oct 2026Greece — Art.5C conditions clarified. The option label and notes now state that the player must have been non-resident in Greece for 5 of the previous 6 years, must move residence from an EU/EEA or tax-cooperation country (US eligibility unconfirmed), needs a Greek employer in a new position and must declare a stay of at least 2 years. The unsupported remark that most foreign EuroLeague players use Art.5C was removed.
  6. 27
    Update, 20 Oct 2026Turkey — three options merged into two. The separate “withholding (≤ ~€99k)” option was removed: it gave the same result as the high-earner option for any salary above the threshold. Both remaining options (local/US with full SGK; EU/UK foreign, SGK-exempt) now apply the 20% final withholding up to ~€98,881 gross and the progressive scale on all income above it. For a net input the calculator solves for the gross that gives that final net and, where two gross amounts give the same net (a band of net amounts just below the threshold-level net), uses the lower one. The “Target net unreachable” message cannot occur with the current numbers (there is an overlap, not a gap).

  14. 19
    October 2026 reviewBasque Country — scale corrected, claim not reproduced. The earlier bracket limits did not match the official 2026 foral scale (23% to €18,080 … 47% to €208,390, then 49%, identical in Álava, Bizkaia and Gipuzkoa). On a multi-million salary the Basque marginal rate (49%) is higher than Madrid’s (45%). The widely quoted “about 25% vs 50%” traces to (a) a 2019 Bizkaia decree exempting 50% of “irregular” income such as athletes’ pay without a cap, which was withdrawn in September 2019; (b) a 2011 comparison of 24% vs 43% from when Bizkaia and Álava let imported players use an impatriate regime (the common regime closed it to athletes in 2015; I could not confirm the foral position); and (c) mixing net-contract and gross-contract budgets. An optional what-if field lets you apply a reduction to test the press claim.
  15. 18
    October 2026 reviewLithuania — income tax corrected. The VMI table for 2026 taxes sportsmen’s income in four marginal bands: 15% to 12 VDU (€27,746), 20% to 36 VDU (€83,237), 25% to 60 VDU (€138,729), 32% above (VDU 2026 = €2,312.15). This supersedes item (1), which used 15% to 120 VDU / 20% above. Sodra: the contribution base is 90% of pay only from 1 July 2026 (50% before) and is capped at 43 VDU (€99,422.45), so local players’ social security now tops out near €22.4k instead of growing without limit.
  16. 17
    Turkey withholding — real bug fixed: the "20%, income ≤ ~€99k" option was computing a flat 20% withholding for any salary size, with no cap actually enforced in the code — the €99k figure only lived in the label text. A €1M net target was incorrectly resolving to a €1.26M gross taxed at a flat 20%, when Turkish law requires the full progressive scale (up to 40%) via mandatory annual declaration above that threshold. Fixed: this option now automatically falls through to the same full-progressive calculation as the high-earner option once gross exceeds ~€98,881/yr, matching the confirmed 20%-stopaj mechanics discussed for the Mike James case. Net-input mode also correctly handles the ~€57k–€69k zone right at the threshold where crossing it makes tax worse (net drops) rather than better — unlike Italy's Impatriati cap, this creates an overlap (two valid gross solutions) rather than a gap, and the calculator automatically resolves to the cheaper, lower-gross one.
  17. 16
    Serbia: the SS contribution cap was corrected from ~€90,000/yr to ~€74,940/yr — the old figure didn't match the official 2026 maximum monthly base (732,820 RSD, published by the PIO fund) converted at the current ~117.35 RSD/€ rate. The mandatory annual surtax (godišnji porez na dohodak građana) is now included directly in the tax calculation rather than left out as a warning-only caveat — it's genuinely material at basketball-level salaries: 10% on net income above roughly €46,354 (3× average annual salary), rising to 15% above about €139,000 total, modeled using the extra under-40 personal deduction that applies to local and foreign players alike (not just Art.15v users). Also corrected the "Art.15v" option's framing: the primary qualifying path for the 70% payroll-tax-base reduction is not having predominantly resided in Serbia for 24 months, with no hard age-40 cutoff on that main path — the under-40 condition only applies to a separate, narrower path for people returning from study abroad.

  19. 14
    Opus review, July 2026Israel — added a third, previously-missing regime: foreign athlete (flat 25%), under the 1998 Foreign Athlete tax regulations. This is separate from both the standard resident scale (up to 50%) and the new-immigrant/oleh-chadash track (which is about becoming an Israeli resident, more relevant to a returning national than a genuine import). Foreign athletes who remain non-residents get a flat 25% rate plus sharply reduced National Insurance (~2% vs. the resident rate), for up to 4 years — this is what produces the commonly-cited ~1.25x gross-to-net multiplier for imports vs. ~2x for local players.
  20. 13
    Opus review, July 2026Italy Impatriati — CORRECTED after further review: the initial rebuild wrongly applied an athlete-specific €1,000,000/yr income floor (with age-20 and 0.5% levy requirements) sourced from art.16 of D.Lgs.147/2015 — but that article was repealed by D.Lgs.209/2023 for anyone transferring Italian residence from 2024 onward, and only survives for players who signed contracts by 31 Dec 2023. The regime now correctly reflects the current D.Lgs.209/2023 art.5 rules that apply to athletes the same as any other qualifying worker: 50% exemption up to a €600,000/yr cap (not a floor) — income above €600k is fully taxed at ordinary rates with no cliff or gap. No age requirement, no 0.5% levy in the current regime.
